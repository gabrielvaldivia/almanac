# Almanac bug audit — September 14, 2026

The audit confirmed **10 product issues and an intermittent subscription-test failure**. The most consequential problems are in recurring-series edits: changing an ending can delete history, moving a monthly series can produce inconsistent dates, and deleting a sparse series’ only stored occurrence loses its future schedule.

The two Naked-style issues reported during this audit are **fixed, pushed, and installed on the connected iPhone** in commit `36b3cca`: rounded backgrounds no longer clip row contents, and Today scrolls the preceding Naked row completely out of view. The remaining findings below are open.

## Scope and evidence

Reviewed the app, shared models/storage, recurrence, notification scheduling, contact birthday import, quick-entry parser and editors, timeline/list synchronization, category management, widget providers/deep links, subscriptions, and the CI workflow. Baseline: `378c1c7`; the only production changes during the audit were the two Naked-style fixes.

- Fresh iPhone 17 Pro / iOS 26.5 simulator, Xcode 26.6: **99 unit tests and 13 UI tests passed** in the initial full run.
- **11 additional probes**: ten reproduced the product issues below; one confirmed custom category repeat defaults already work. These intentionally assert observed failure modes, so their passing status means reproduction, not correctness. Source: [audit reproductions](2026-09-14-reproduction.swift).
- A separate simulator fixture and UI check verified Today leaves the prior Naked row outside the viewport. The screenshot was inspected for unclipped color bars, wrapped titles, and the list’s top edge.
- Release device build succeeded; installation and launch succeeded on the connected iPhone. Test fixture data stayed on disposable simulators.
- [GitHub run 34848052198](https://github.com/gabrielvaldivia/almanac/actions/runs/34848052198), for baseline `378c1c7`, failed the subscription entitlement test; UI tests were skipped. A local three-iteration `test-without-building` run reproduced the same failure **twice**, with one pass.

This is not a clean bill of health despite the initial full-suite pass. Existing tests miss these edge cases, and the subscription test is intermittent.

## Confirmed product findings

### 1. Changing a repeat ending deletes history

**Reproduction:** Generate an indefinite daily series beginning January 1, 2024 and retain its stored 2024 occurrences. Change only its ending to January 1, 2030, applying the edit to the series. The previously stored 2024 events disappear.

**Cause:** `updatingSeries` regenerates the series using `generate` with the current time. The generator deliberately skips old history for newly materialized indefinite/on-date schedules; the edit then replaces every original series member with that shortened result. An ending before the current generation window can also leave no generated members.

**Fix:** Separate new-series materialization from editing an existing series. Preserve surviving historical occurrences, IDs, and exceptions when changing an ending; prune only occurrences excluded by the new ending.

Source: [Recurrence.swift](../../Up%20Next/Shared/Recurrence.swift), lines 94–97 and 207–228. Probe: `testChangingOnlyEndDateDropsStoredHistory`.

### 2. Moving a monthly series produces dates that disagree with its rule

**Reproduction:** Create a three-occurrence monthly series on January 31, 2030: January 31, February 28, March 31. Move the first occurrence to January 30 and apply to all. Stored dates become January 30, **February 27**, March 30, while the updated monthly rule computes **February 28** for its second occurrence.

**Cause:** The unchanged-pattern branch shifts every stored date by the same number of days through the legacy updater. Calendar-month recurrence cannot be represented by that uniform shift around short months.

**Fix:** Recalculate ordinary occurrences from the adjusted anchor and occurrence index, preserving their IDs and explicit exception semantics. Test month ends and February 29 yearly schedules.

Source: [Recurrence.swift](../../Up%20Next/Shared/Recurrence.swift), lines 188–204; [Models.swift](../../Up%20Next/Shared/Models.swift), `EventSeries.updatingLegacy`. Probe: `testMonthlyMoveStoresADateThatDisagreesWithItsRule`.

### 3. Invalid dates in supported formats silently default to Today

**Reproduction:** Enter `Party 31 February` or `Party Sept 32`. Parsing fails, but the composer resolves the date to Today and does not require schedule review. The invalid text remains in the event title. By comparison, `Party 2/30` correctly requires correction.

**Cause:** The invalid-date hint recognizes numeric and most month-first forms, but misses day-first dates and the `Sept` abbreviation, even though the parser supports those formats.

**Fix:** Have parsing distinguish “no date supplied” from “date supplied but invalid,” using a shared grammar rather than a second partial regular expression. Require correction for the latter.

Source: [NewEventDraft.swift](../../Up%20Next/Up%20Next/NewEventDraft.swift), lines 92–106. Probe: `testInvalidSupportedDateSyntaxSilentlyDefaultsToToday`.

### 4. Renaming a category resets custom event colors

**Reproduction:** Give an event in the blue Work category an orange override. Rename Work to Career without changing the category’s color. The event becomes blue.

**Cause:** Every category save calls `updateEventsForCategoryChange`, which unconditionally assigns `newColor` to all matching events, including name-only saves.

**Fix:** Separate renaming from recoloring. Preserve overrides during renames; when changing a category color, update only events that inherit that color, using explicit inheritance metadata if necessary.

Source: [AppData.swift](../../Up%20Next/Up%20Next/AppData.swift), lines 288–300; [CategoriesView.swift](../../Up%20Next/Up%20Next/CategoriesView.swift), edit callback. Probe: `testCategoryRenameOverwritesAnEventsCustomColor`.

### 5. Category editing bypasses event-storage recovery protection

**Reproduction:** Save a Work event, make the event payload unreadable, and load it to enter recovery mode. Rename Work to Career. Categories persist immediately, while `saveEvents` refuses to write. Restore the readable event payload: its event still references Work, which no longer exists. Category deletion has the same split-write pattern.

**Cause:** Category editing checks only category-storage errors. The dependent event update is blocked independently, after the category mutation has already persisted.

**Fix:** Block category rename/delete when event storage is unavailable, and commit dependent category/event changes together or roll them back together on failure. Keep the recovery error visible.

Source: [CategoryForm.swift](../../Up%20Next/Up%20Next/CategoryForm.swift), Save validation; [CategoriesView.swift](../../Up%20Next/Up%20Next/CategoriesView.swift), rename/delete; [AppData.swift](../../Up%20Next/Up%20Next/AppData.swift), lines 200–205. Probe: `testCategoryRenameWhileEventsAreUnreadableLeavesDanglingCategory`.

### 6. Timeline Today uses a stale anchor after a time-zone change

**Reproduction:** Construct the timeline in New York, change the process’s current zone to Los Angeles, update events, and invoke Today. The resulting focused date resolves to the preceding local calendar day.

**Cause:** The timeline keeps its original absolute midnight anchor for its entire lifetime but computes subsequent offsets with `Calendar.current`. The original midnight is no longer midnight after travel. Its update cache also does not include the current calendar/day/time zone.

**Fix:** Store the anchor as a calendar day and rebase/reindex on significant time or calendar changes, preserving the visible calendar date. Invalidate the date-axis and Today caches even when event contents have not changed. Add a midnight-boundary check alongside the reproduced travel case.

Source: [EventTimelineView.swift](../../Up%20Next/Up%20Next/EventTimelineView.swift), lines 292, 391–407, and 509–516. Probe: `testTimelineTodayFocusPointsToYesterdayAfterWestwardTimeZoneChange`.

This finding concerns timeline dates and focus. It does not prove that an already queued notification fires on the wrong day.

### 7. Deleting one sparse occurrence loses the whole future schedule

**Reproduction:** Create an indefinite event repeating every two years from January 1, 2030. Only that occurrence is materialized within the one-year horizon. Choose “Delete this event.” Replenishing in 2032 produces nothing: the future schedule is gone.

**Cause:** Recurrence rules live only on materialized event records. Deletion stores the excluded occurrence index on remaining members; when there are none, there is nowhere to preserve the rule.

**Fix:** Persist recurring-series definitions independently of occurrence rows, with exclusions and exceptions keyed by occurrence index. Migrate existing embedded rules. Deleting an occurrence must retain the series even when no visible/materialized occurrences remain.

Source: [Recurrence.swift](../../Up%20Next/Shared/Recurrence.swift), lines 87, 127–133, and 163–172. Probe: `testDeletingOnlyMaterializedOccurrenceErasesIndefiniteSchedule`.

### 8. A future widget event may not open when tapped

**Reproduction:** Replenish the same stored yearly-series snapshot independently as a widget and as the app, at a date beyond the stored horizon. Both create the same future calendar occurrence, but its UUID differs. The widget’s event URL contains an ID absent from the app’s list.

**Cause:** Widgets replenish without saving, and newly generated occurrences receive random UUIDs. The app independently replenishes on activation. The Next Event widget links by UUID; the app silently ignores an event link it cannot resolve.

**Fix:** Give generated occurrences stable identity derived from series and occurrence index, or deep-link by that pair and resolve/materialize it in the app. Preserve existing stored IDs during migration.

Source: [EventLoader.swift](../../Up%20Next/UpNextWidget/EventLoader.swift), [Recurrence.swift](../../Up%20Next/Shared/Recurrence.swift), line 107; [UpNextWidget.swift](../../Up%20Next/UpNextWidget/UpNextWidget.swift), Next Event link; [ContentView.swift](../../Up%20Next/Up%20Next/ContentView.swift), `handleOpenURL`. Probe: `testIndependentWidgetAndAppRefillsGenerateDifferentDeepLinkIDs`.

### 9. Renaming a category breaks an existing widget filter

**Reproduction:** Configure a widget for Work, then rename Work to Career. The widget configuration still supplies `Work`; the renamed events now carry `Career`, so the widget’s filter returns no events.

**Cause:** Widget configuration uses the category’s editable name as identity. Reloading timelines does not migrate the saved intent parameter.

**Fix:** Introduce stable category IDs for widget selection and persist a migration/alias path for existing string-based configurations. Define a useful fallback when a selected category is deleted.

Source: [AppIntent.swift](../../Up%20Next/UpNextWidget/AppIntent.swift), `category`; [WidgetEvents.swift](../../Up%20Next/Shared/WidgetEvents.swift), `upcoming`. Probe: `testRenamingCategoryEmptiesExistingWidgetFilter`.

### 10. Cross-year ranges omit both years

**Reproduction:** A December 31, 2026–January 2, 2027 event, viewed in 2026, displays `Thu, Dec 31 → Sat, Jan 2 (3 days)` with no year.

**Cause:** The shared formatter chooses whether to include a year solely from the start date’s relationship to the current year. The composer’s range pill already handles this more carefully, so the two presentations disagree.

**Fix:** Include year context whenever the endpoints cross years, using a shared formatting policy across composer, rows, and widgets.

Source: [EventDateText.swift](../../Up%20Next/Shared/EventDateText.swift), lines 4–11. Probe: `testRangeCrossingNewYearOmitsBothYears`.

## Subscription / CI investigation

The failure is `SubscriptionTests.testVerifiedPurchaseAndRefundRefreshEntitlements`, at line 41: **“The verified purchase did not grant the entitlement.”** Product loading and verified purchase succeed; `isSubscribed` does not become true within the subsequent 30-second observation window. The refund portion is therefore never reached on failed runs.

The initial full local suite passed. Repeating the existing test three times with `test-without-building` produced one pass followed by two failures. This confirms an intermittent test/entitlement-state problem, but does **not** establish that production App Store purchases fail.

Next diagnostic step: record transaction IDs, revocation/expiration state, entitlement-stream results, and refresh revision ordering around the test-session reset, purchase, and transaction listener. Determine whether session state survives between runs or the app publishes a stale entitlement result. Fix the cause and require repeated purchase/refund passes; do not hide it with skipped tests or larger timeouts. Run UI tests independently of unit success so one failing integration test does not erase all UI coverage for that commit.

Sources: [SubscriptionTests.swift](../../Up%20Next/Up%20NextTests/SubscriptionTests.swift), [AppData.swift](../../Up%20Next/Up%20Next/AppData.swift), subscription refresh/listener; [workflow](../../.github/workflows/ios-tests.yml).

## Lower-priority redundancy and consistency work

- Remove unreferenced `EditEventView.deleteSingleEvent`, `getCategoryColor`, and its private formatter; remove the unused formatter in `EventRow` and the unreferenced rounded-font extension. Verify references across app and widget targets before deleting helpers.
- Consolidate widget grouping/sorting duplicated across medium/large layouts. It currently converts relative labels back into numbers and treats those day counts as seconds solely to sort them. Order by calendar dates directly; keep formatted labels as presentation.
- Rename the surviving `EventSheet…` synchronization types to list terminology now that the draggable sheet has been removed. This is maintenance clarity, not a runtime bug.
- Consolidate date parsing/validation and shared range formatting as part of findings 3 and 10; those duplicated policies already caused observable inconsistencies.

## Notification coverage and remaining limits

The current notification tests passed for calendar-day scheduling, queue limits, ongoing events, local-time/date handling, and ordered replacement of pending requests. The audit did not reproduce a new scheduling defect independent of the event-data issues above. Wrong or missing recurring event records can still lead to wrong or missing reminders.

The known finite queue remains: the app schedules up to 64 event-days and replenishes on activation, significant time changes, and best-effort background refresh. This run did not validate real delivery over many days while the app remains unopened, Focus/notification-summary behavior, or OS background scheduling. Real purchase/refund behavior and contacts permission changes on the user’s phone were not exercised; subscription checks used StoreKit Test and contacts UI checks used fixtures.
